B2C commerce · Onboarding · Product thinking

Make the first useful action obvious.

A generic home screen makes a chronic-care customer do all the work of finding a relevant path. This prototype tests a narrower idea: let a customer choose their care areas, then make relevant commerce categories and the next action easier to find.

01 / Identify

What are you shopping for?

Select one or more care areas. This is a navigation preference, not a diagnosis.

Your next step appears here.

Select at least one care area to preview the personalised journey.

02 / Product decision

Keep the journey narrow

IdentifyAsk only for information that changes the next screen.
SelectSupport popular choices, browse-all and multi-select.
PersonaliseShow matching categories and explain why they appear.
ActSend the customer to a relevant existing commerce destination.

Problem: the default homepage treats different customer intents the same. Product hypothesis: a small amount of explicit intent can reduce navigation friction. This demo does not claim a measured conversion lift.

03 / Inspect the implementation

Architecture

Customer selection→Client-side state→Category mapping→Next-action preview

No server, analytics SDK, medical inference, or customer data storage is used. The state lives in the page and disappears on refresh. In a real experiment, measure selection completion, time to first relevant category, CTA clicks, drop-off and downstream conversion against a control cohort.

Truemeds · Onboarding · Android

What if the first login understood why the customer came?

The generic homepage made a chronic-care customer navigate the same experience as everyone else. The product opportunity was to identify the customer's condition early, then make the next useful action obvious.

The flow was intentionally narrow: identify → select → personalise → act. It was not a disease-management product; it was an onboarding layer that connected the customer's intent to the existing commerce journey.

PlatformAndroid
Experiment20% vs 80%
SelectionTop 8 + A–Z + search
Core behaviourCondition → CTA
01 · Identify — first-time customer enters the chronic onboarding experience.
02 · Select — choose a condition using popular diseases, A–Z browsing, search and multi-select chips.
03 · Personalise — show disease-specific savings, categories, benefits and relevant content.
04 · Act — guide the customer through the appropriate CTA/deep link into the existing journey.

Product decision map

Experience
Generic homepage → condition-led journey

The product earns the right to personalise by asking one useful question early.

Scope — Android first-login only; returning users, iOS/web and disease-management functionality stayed out.
Content — backend-configured disease content with fallback assets so the experience could evolve without an app release for every content change.
Measurement — selection, search/A–Z interaction, CTA clicks, drop-offs and downstream conversion.
Experiment — persistent 20% onboarding cohort vs 80% control, allowing the team to compare the journey against the unchanged homepage.

AI agent · Operations

How much should an agent actually be allowed to do?

The interesting product problem is not “can the model answer?” It is deciding which actions are safe to automate, which need evidence, and which should always stop at a human.

Intent — classify the incoming request.
Context — retrieve only the information required.
Policy — check whether the requested action is permitted.
Confidence — route uncertain or high-risk cases to people.
Action — execute low-risk work or escalate with context.

How I think

I don't believe good product work is about having the cleverest idea. It's about choosing the right problem, making the trade-offs explicit and knowing what success should look like.

Customer

What is the user trying to accomplish? Where is the friction? What behaviour are we actually trying to change?

Business

What's the economic engine? Revenue, retention, conversion, cost, acquisition — and which metric is genuinely causal?

Technology

What can we ship reliably? Where do APIs, data, architecture or operational constraints change the product decision?

College product project · Reconstructed 2026

How might an independent artist grow monthly listeners without simply buying more reach?

This was a college project I built around a simple growth problem for Spotify artists: getting discovered is only useful if discovery turns into actual listening, and listening turns into repeat behaviour.

I no longer have the original project files, so I’m deliberately not inventing screenshots, research quotes or historical results. Instead, I’ve reconstructed the product thinking from the problem I remember.

Growth loop
01
Discovery — get the right listener to the artist
02
First listen — reduce the friction to try a track
03
Repeat — create reasons to return
The north-star question: does the product create more monthly listeners, not merely more impressions?

What I'd measure today: artist discovery → first play conversion, repeat-listen rate, listeners retained over 28 days, playlist/source contribution and incremental monthly listeners.

Why this belongs here: it shows the origin of my interest in growth product — taking an audience problem and turning it into a measurable product loop.

How I think

I don't believe good product work is about having the cleverest idea. It's about choosing the right problem, making the trade-offs explicit and knowing what success should look like.

Customer

What is the user trying to accomplish? Where is the friction? What behaviour are we actually trying to change?

Business

What's the economic engine? Revenue, retention, conversion, cost, acquisition — and which metric is genuinely causal?

Technology

What can we ship reliably? Where do APIs, data, architecture or operational constraints change the product decision?
